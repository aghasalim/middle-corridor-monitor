### Published uses of Sentinel-1 or other imagery

   Sentinel-1 radar points near the Volga-Caspian Canal and the Iranian ports of Anzali and Amirabad, July 2022 to June 2023 against earlier years, with ports in Azerbaijan, Turkmenistan and Kazakhstan as a comparison. ## 4. What this changes for the paper

**Does any event explain the Ro-Ro step?** No. I found no real-world event between July 2021 and January 2022 that would raise Ro-Ro calls four- to sixfold at Baku, Aktau and Turkmenbashi together. The evidence against a real cause is moderate, not conclusive, because it rests on not finding things.

1. **Fleet.** No Ro-Ro-registered ship joined any Caspian fleet in 2021 or 2022. The two new ships are Passenger/Ro-Ro. They entered service in May 2021 and May 2022, not at the fitted breaks (November 2021 to January 2022). "Zarifa Aliyeva" made 163 trips in its first year. If a trip is one crossing, that is about 80 calls a year at Baku per ship, so the two together could add about 160 calls a year at Baku against a step of about 380. So even if PortWatch counts Passenger/Ro-Ro ships, which is undocumented, the new ships explain less than half of the Baku step, and the timing is wrong. This can be tested in the daily data: look for a rise at Baku in May 2021 and another in May 2022.
2. **COVID.** The freight ferry lines were restricted, not closed. Kazakhstan's March 2020 measure exempted trucks with crew, and trucks and railcars kept moving on the Alat to Kuryk line. The dated reopenings I found are for Turkmenistan's road transit (1 June 2022) and its list of open border points (November 2022), both after the break. PortWatch Ro-Ro calls also do not fall in 2020. So a reopening does not explain the step. I found no dated restriction on the Alat to Turkmenbashi ferry itself after April 2020, so this part is weak.
3. **Renaming.** Two ASCO ferries have name records starting February 2021: the Ro-Ro-registered AKADEMIK ZARIFA ALIYEVA became NAKHCHYVAN, and a Passenger/Ro-Ro ferry became ORDUBAD. The likely reason is that the old name was wanted for the new Ro-Pax launched on 1 March 2021; that reason is my inference. February 2021 falls inside the early-2021 trough. A renamed ship can drop out of an AIS series if the provider matches ships to a registry by name or MMSI. But this touches one Ro-Ro-registered ship out of seven, and the trough also shows at Poti. So it is at most a small part of the trough and does not explain the later step. Evidence: weak.
4. **Shore AIS.** Turkmenbashi has a VTS with an AIS base station, but the date is unknown and a port's own base station does not feed Spire or FleetMon unless shared. Aktau's VTS was still planned in 2024. Nothing found for Baku. Evidence: none for a 2021–22 change.
5. **AIS supply.** The one dated event inside the break window is Spire's purchase of exactEarth (court order 22 November 2021, delisting about 2 December 2021). This is a company date, not a data-feed date. It stays the leading candidate and stays unverified, as the paper says.
