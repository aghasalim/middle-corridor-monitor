Note 1. The strategy table is not a fully independent source: AMTP most likely took it from the same Maritime
Administration data. What it adds is that another publisher, in 2022, put 1,560 kt against Kuryk and 667 kt against the
North Terminal. The pie chart beside it (61% / 16% / 23%) matches neither year, so the page is not internally tidy.

Note 2. The independent evidence is the in-year reporting. By the end of October 2020 Kuryk had already handled about
1,352 kt, and by the end of May 562 kt on the Alat route alone. A full-year figure of 666.8 kt for Kuryk is therefore
impossible. The North Terminal had 544 kt after ten months, which fits a full year of 667 kt and not 1,560 kt.

Not found: a full-year 2020 press figure for Kuryk from the port, KTZ, the ministry or Kazinform; any independent count

### What this changes for the paper

- The transposition in the stat.gov.kz table is now supported by three opened sources. Section 5.6 can say that Kuryk
  handled about 1,560 kt in 2020 and AMCT about 667 kt, and that the published table has the two swapped. "We could not
  confirm this at the source" should be replaced by the evidence above. It is still true that the Maritime
  Administration has not corrected its table.
- Table 4 can show 1,560.1 kt and 29.2% for 2020 as the main reading, with the published 666.8 kt in the footnote. The
  abstract's "one year is in doubt" can go. The Kuryk share is then 18% to 30% without a caveat.
- The footnote under Table 3 (Aktau + AMCT tonnes with the 2020 values exchanged) becomes the main row.
- The 2020 ferry-call count for Kuryk (542) has no second source. Vessel calls were not swapped as far as can be told:
  566 calls with 542 ferries fits a ferry port.
- New contradiction to list in the README: transit through Kazakh seaports in 2020 is 842.1 kt in the ministry's
  programme report and 389.1 kt in the Maritime Administration table (2019: 470 against 275).

### What this changes for the paper

- Section 2 says the 1.867 Mt figure "was seen only in a search snippet". It is now verified on the Association's home
  page, with 1,857 kt for 2024 next to it. "Slightly above 2024" is therefore right.
- Section 2 says "For 2024 the association published 3.3 Mt". That is still true of its March 2025 release, but its own
  chart now shows 1,857 kt for 2024. The paper should give both and say they conflict.
- Section 3.2 says the ministry series is "the only one with seven consecutive years". That is no longer true. The
  Association's chart has nine consecutive years (2017–2025) in tonnes and in TEU.
- Table 3 and section 5.4 use the ministry series as "the route total". The two series are identical for 2019–2022, so
  the choice matters only for 2023–2025, but those are three of the six year-on-year changes. The route-total rows
  should be run on both series and both reported. Until that is done, the correlations of −0.05, 0.79 and 0.80 hold
  for the ministry series only.
- The README's guess that the 1.867 Mt figure is "probably the cargo carried under the Association's coordinated route
  or tariffs" stays unverified. What can be said is narrower: the Association publishes a lower series than the ministry
  from 2023, and neither is defined.
- The 2023 container dip (33.6 → 20.5 thousand TEU) is on the Association's own chart, so it is not a reporting gap.
- The ministry rows for 2019–2025 in the CSV were `step1`; the bizmedia.kz page and the gov.kz item were opened today.

### What this changes for the paper

- Section 2 and section 6 say the World Bank's September 2026 report "was not opened". It is now opened. It uses no
  AIS or satellite data, so the sentence "We found no use of ship-tracking (AIS) or satellite data in any of them" can
  cover it too. The claim rests on a text search, which should be said.
- The 2026 report adds a fourth basis for "how much crosses the Caspian": 8.8 Mt in 2023 including tanker oil, or
  3.5 Mt without oil. This is a model estimate for three ports (Baku, Aktau, Turkmenbashi), not a count. Against it
  stand the ministry's 2.764 Mt and the Association's 1.629 Mt (chart) or 2.7 Mt (release) for the same year.
- The World Bank itself takes its corridor growth rates from the Association's website, and the rates match the lower
  chart series (586 → 1,867 kt). So the newest institutional report does not use the ministry's 4.1 Mt.
- Section 5.3: the Alat split for 2022 (6.3 Mt; 2.5 on railcars, 1.1 in trucks) is confirmed, with a third part of
  2.7 Mt through the multi-purpose terminal. Section 5.6: the fleet count (15 rail ferries, 2 ro-ro, 2 ro-pax) is
  confirmed; the same passage adds 15 conventional dry cargo vessels.
- New for the validation file: Port of Baku full-year 2022 containers (52,277 TEU), which the README lists as not
  found; and the World Bank's statement that Kuryk's cargo is 79% trucks and wagons to Baku.
- The World Bank's Aktau figure for 2023 (4.5 Mt) is the port company alone and agrees with the CSV (4,483 kt).
- Nothing in these reports gives a monthly series or a ferry-call count.
